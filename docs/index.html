How the headline forecast works
  1. Allocate the observed 2024 presidential vote into each 2026 legislative district.
  2. Apply the post-2016 national-environment ramp.
  3. Blend 80% of that ramp with 20% of a regularized model using incumbency, fundraising, demographics, presidential trend, chamber, and realignment-era variables.
  4. Simulate shared statewide, chamber, and district uncertainty.

How to read this forecast

The public headline is an 80/20 ensemble: 80% of the post-2016 environment ramp and 20% of a cycle-balanced ridge residual model.

Open any modeled district to see every input value, its signed effect, the running margin, and the final reconciliation. Nonlinear-model decompositions use a fixed sequential reveal order, so their individual effects are descriptive and order-dependent.

Experimental uncertainty estimates.

District probabilities and seat ranges are calculated from 50,000 simulations around the poll-adjusted baseline. Each simulation includes an Alabama-wide error shared by every district, an additional House or Senate error shared within that chamber, and district-specific error.

The error scales use the full 1994–2022 archive and expanding-window holdouts from 1998 through 2022. The archive contains only eight distinct election environments, so estimated correlations and probabilities remain provisional rather than fully calibrated.

Districts with only one major-party nominee are treated as fixed Democratic or Republican seats in chamber summaries. Independent-only and otherwise unresolved districts remain unmodeled and are displayed in gray.

The national-environment projection uses polling through the date shown above and should be refreshed as new polling becomes available.
