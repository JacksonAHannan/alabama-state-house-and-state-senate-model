Candidate Margin Overperformance

A retrospective index of how far an Alabama legislative candidate ran ahead of or behind a cross-fitted expectation for the district and election.

1. What CMO measures

CMO is candidate margin overperformance, not literal wins above replacement and not a causal estimate of candidate quality. It compares the candidate’s observed two-party margin with a statistical expectation based on the political and demographic context of the race.

Democratic CMO = observed Democratic two-party margin − cross-fitted expected Democratic margin
Republican CMO = − Democratic CMO

A Democratic candidate who was expected to lose by 20 points but lost by 10 has a CMO of +10. The Republican in that race receives −10. Scores are therefore zero-sum within a race and cannot separately identify both candidates’ contributions.

2. Data and eligibility

The index covers all eight Alabama legislative general-election cycles from 1994 through 2022. The 1998–2022 series is the core historical tier; 1994 is retained and visibly flagged as a sensitivity tier because its presidential and split-precinct allocations rely more heavily on fallbacks. A race is scored only when both major parties received votes.

  • Official and reconciled election returns provide candidates, parties, and votes.
  • Same-cycle statewide offices and preceding presidential returns provide political context.
  • Decennial Census and ACS sources provide era-appropriate demographics.
  • Incumbency uses positive evidence; ambiguous dual matches are neutralized and flagged.
  • Finance is an optional sensitivity layer rather than a requirement for headline CMO.

3. The expected baseline

The model predicts Democratic legislative overperformance relative to a same-cycle statewide-office index. Predictors describe the district and election rather than simply restating the legislative outcome: party-specific incumbency, prior presidential performance and trend, demographics, chamber, cycle, and source-availability indicators. Numeric missing values are imputed within the modeling pipeline and explicit availability flags preserve missingness information.

Why a regularized model? Ridge regression shrinks unstable coefficients in a small, correlated dataset. It is intentionally modest: CMO is the unexplained remainder after the model establishes a reasonable contextual expectation.

4. Out-of-fold scoring

The published headline score is out-of-fold. Each race is predicted by a model that did not train on that observation, and regularization is selected inside the training data. This avoids ranking candidates by an expectation fitted directly to their own result.

The model also withholds whole election cycles. Those leave-one-cycle-out results test how sensitive a score is to historical era changes. The displayed stability band summarizes this cross-cycle sensitivity; it is not labeled or interpreted as a 95% confidence interval.

5. Total and resource-adjusted CMO

Total CMO, the headline measure, excludes spending. It is closer to total campaign overperformance because fundraising may itself reflect candidate strength. Resource-adjusted CMO includes relative campaign spending and a finance-availability flag, asking how a candidate performed after accounting for observed resources.

Neither version supports a causal claim. Fundraising is endogenous, finance coverage is incomplete, and money raised is not the same as money efficiently deployed.

6. Validation and interpretation

Validation emphasizes forward and grouped tests rather than random folds alone. The model reports random out-of-fold error, leave-one-cycle-out error, source coverage, exact vote-total checks, score symmetry, and sensitivity to specifications. Historical era shifts—especially 2014—make cycle holdouts materially harder than within-era prediction.

Large positive scores mean “far ahead of this model’s expectation,” not “personally caused this many points.” Rankings are most useful alongside the district result, expected baseline, top-of-ticket context, stability band, and source notes.

7. Important limitations

  • The index contains eight cycles and 511 eligible races, but only a small number of independent election environments.
  • Scores are conditional on contested Democratic-versus-Republican races and do not represent all candidates or legislators.
  • The zero-sum construction attributes a race residual symmetrically to the two candidates.
  • Election eras, district boundaries, turnout, and source quality change across cycles; 1994 is a sensitivity tier.
  • Same-cycle context makes the historical index descriptive; it cannot be used unchanged before Election Day.
  • The stability band is a model-sensitivity diagnostic, not calibrated predictive uncertainty.

8. Relationship to the 2026 forecast

The forecast and CMO are separate products. CMO describes historical overperformance. For 2026, prior CMO was tested as a shrinkage-adjusted candidate layer using exact candidate-and-party matches. It did not improve both the average and latest forward-cycle error under the declared promotion rule, so it does not alter the headline forecast and remains a scenario layer.
