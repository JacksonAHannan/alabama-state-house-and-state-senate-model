Forecast methodology

How national polling, federal partisanship, incumbency, and fundraising produce the district forecasts and chamber probabilities.

Headline margin

Headline margin = 2024 presidential margin + national polling swing + expected Alabama legislative overperformance

The first two terms represent the federal result implied by current polling. The final term estimates how an Alabama legislative candidate would normally run relative to that federal baseline given the race’s chamber, incumbency, and fundraising position.

National environment

The national two-party generic-ballot environment is D+7.55 across 6 quality-gated pollsters through 2026-08-17. Compared with the 2024 national presidential result, this produces a +9.03-point Democratic swing that is applied uniformly to every district’s 2024 presidential margin.

Down-ballot and candidate adjustment

The model learns the legislative-minus-federal margin from elections after 2016. It estimates a generic Alabama legislative lag, an incumbency adjustment, and the Democratic-versus-Republican fundraising gap.

Fundraising is normalized within the election cycle. A first-stage model estimates the fundraising gap normally associated with district partisanship, competitiveness, chamber, and incumbency. The forecast uses the remaining relative fundraising strength. This first stage never uses the legislative result.

Explicit Alabama campaign-finance observations are complete for 43 of 48 contested two-party races. A race without complete finance receives the lag and incumbency terms but no fundraising adjustment.

Historical test

The model trains on 59 contested races in 2018 and predicts the same 30 contested 2022 races under every specification.

The headline specification records 7.08 points of mean absolute margin error, compared with 10.00 for the polling-implied federal baseline. A paired race bootstrap estimates a 2.91-point improvement, with a 95% interval from 0.83 to 5.00 points.

Probabilities and chamber summaries

Expected margins are converted to conditional win probabilities with a Student-t curve with five degrees of freedom and a 5.75-point scale. The headline uses 50,000 simulations with shared national (2.20), statewide (2.01), and chamber (1.03) errors plus district-specific error (5.91).

Single-major-party districts are fixed in chamber totals, even if an independent is present. Unresolved districts remain unmodeled.

Dem and Rep scenarios

The Dem scenario shifts every district 2.20 points Democratic. The Rep scenario shifts every district 2.20 points Republican. That amount is one historical national polling-error standard deviation; all candidate and district adjustments otherwise remain unchanged.

Limitations

  • The complete candidate adjustment has one direct forward test, from 2018 to 2022.
  • Historical fundraising covers full election cycles, while the current 2026 figures are a partial-cycle snapshot.
  • Fundraising reflects resources, donor expectations, and candidate strength and should not be interpreted as a purely causal campaign effect.
  • Alabama polling is sparse, so the national generic ballot supplies the political-environment estimate.
  • Polling house effects and mode effects are not yet estimated explicitly.
