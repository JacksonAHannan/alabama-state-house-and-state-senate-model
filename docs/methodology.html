Basic and Fundamentals+

Two views of the same CMO expected-performance model, distinguished by how strongly the expectation is applied.

The two views

Basic = poll-adjusted presidential baseline + 20% of the CMO expected-performance adjustment

Basic is the default guardrail against overfitting. It uses the same CMO expectation as Fundamentals+, but shrinks the district adjustment four-fifths of the way toward zero. The current generic-ballot environment is D+7.55 across 6 quality-gated pollsters.

Fundamentals+ = poll-adjusted presidential baseline + 100% of the CMO expected-performance adjustment

The CMO expectation uses district racial and educational composition, prior presidential trend and its availability, chamber, and 2008/2016 era terms. It deliberately excludes candidate incumbency, fundraising, ideology, and prior CMO so downstream consequences of candidate strength are not mistaken for independently known candidate effects.

National environment

The polling signal comes from quality-gated generic-ballot polling. Historical tests support transferring none of the swing before 2018, half in 2018, and the full swing in 2022 and 2026. The 125% continued-nationalization case remains a separate experimental scenario, not a selected model.

Fundamentals+ applies the complete historical expectation and is the richer substantive view. Basic remains the default because its 20% adjustment is a transparent hedge against selection noise and structural change in a small number of election cycles.

Probabilities and chamber simulations

Each view uses its own expanding-window errors in 50,000 deterministic-seed simulations with shared statewide, chamber, and district-specific components. Switching views updates margins, ratings, probabilities, intervals, and chamber summaries together.

Limitations

  • Only a small number of forward election environments—and two after 2016—identify these relationships.
  • The CMO expectation is contextual; it does not identify an individual candidate effect.
  • National race and education crosstabs are not joint race-by-education samples and Alabama polling remains sparse.
  • Polling house effects and mode effects are not yet estimated explicitly.
  • Probabilities remain provisional rather than fully calibrated long-run frequencies.
