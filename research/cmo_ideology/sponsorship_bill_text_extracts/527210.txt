SYNOPSIS:         This bill would consolidate certain8
preemption language regarding the authority of9
counties and municipalities to regulate certain10
activity related to firearms and would provide11
civil remedies for persons adversely affected by12
unauthorized action of a county or municipality13
relating to firearm regulation.14
This bill would provide for the possession15
of a pistol in a motor vehicle and would provide16
that the mere presence of a pistol in a vehicle17
does not create a presumption that the pistol is18
concealed.19
This bill would provide that lawfully20
carrying a firearm under certain conditions does21
not, in and of itself, constitute the crime of22
disorderly conduct.23
This bill would require a sheriff to issue24
or deny a concealed pistol permit within 30 days of25
receipt of the application and accompanying fees,26
would specify that the license must be renewed from27
Page 1

one to five years from the date of issuance, would1
provide for the revocation of licenses, and would2
provide an appeals process for license denials and3
revocations.4
This bill would specify certain eligibility5
requirements for the issuance of a pistol permit6
and would further provide for the permit fee.7
This bill would provide for the issuance of8
pistol permits to applicants who are not United9
States citizens under certain conditions and would10
authorize employers to restrict employees from11
carrying concealed pistols under limited12
circumstances.13
This bill would require a report from the14
National Instant Background Check System as part of15
the investigation process relating to the issuance16
of a concealed pistol permit.17
This bill would authorize the Attorney18
General to enter into reciprocal agreements with19
other states for the mutual recognition of licenses20
to carry pistols.21
This bill would further provide for the22
carrying of a pistol on public and private property23
in certain locations.24
This bill would allow employees to transport25
or store a firearm in the employee's26
privately-owned motor vehicle under certain27
Page 2

conditions and would prohibit an employer from1
inquiring whether the employee is transporting or2
storing a firearm in a vehicle and would provide3
certain remedies for employees.4
This bill would require state and local5
entities to allow certain persons on property of6
the state or local entity to check a firearm at a7
designated entrance for secure storage while the8
person is on the property.9
This bill would specify that a person or10
business entity has no duty to guard against the11
criminal acts of a third party relating to firearm12
use or otherwise.13
This bill would prohibit a business entity14
or property owner or legal possessor from15
establishing policies against persons transporting16
or storing a firearm or ammunition when the person17
is otherwise in compliance with all other18
applicable laws under certain conditions and would19
provide certain remedies for violations.20
This bill would repeal the prohibition21
against the possession of a firearm at a public22
demonstration.23
Amendment 621 of the Constitution of Alabama24
of 1901, now appearing as Section 111.05 of the25
Official Recompilation of the Constitution of26
Alabama of 1901, as amended, prohibits a general27
Page 3

law whose purpose or effect would be to require a1
new or increased expenditure of local funds from2
becoming effective with regard to a local3
governmental entity without enactment by a 2/3 vote4
unless: it comes within one of a number of5
specified exceptions; it is approved by the6
affected entity; or the Legislature appropriates7
funds, or provides a local source of revenue, to8
the entity for the purpose.9
The purpose or effect of this bill would be10
to require a new or increased expenditure of local11
funds within the meaning of the amendment. However,12
the bill does not require approval of a local13
governmental entity or enactment by a 2/3 vote to14
become effective because it comes within one of the15
specified exceptions contained in the amendment.16
