# Working Substack outline: how Alabama Democrats outran their party

## Provisional headline

**The Last Local Democrats: How Alabama Candidates Outran a Collapsing Party**

Alternative: **It Wasn't Just Moderation: The Three Ways Alabama Democrats
Overperformed**

## Central claim

Alabama Democratic overperformance since 2010 did not come from one ideological
formula. In white rural districts, culturally conservative positions often
helped candidates preserve crossover support, but they worked as part of a
larger bundle: economic populism, public-school advocacy, local roots, and a
personal identity distinct from the national party. Long-serving Democrats in
Black-majority districts and progressive candidates in changing suburbs
overperformed through different coalitions.

## Narrative structure

### 1. Open with the apparent answer

Begin with Johnny Mack Morrow or Larry Means: pro-life/pro-gun or NRA-aligned
Democrats who substantially outran the statewide Democratic ticket. Establish
why “they were conservative Democrats” feels like the obvious explanation.

### 2. Explain the measure without overselling it

- CMO is two-party legislative margin relative to a contextual model
  expectation, expressed in percentage points.
- It is historical candidate overperformance, not wins above replacement and
  not a causal candidate-quality estimate.
- Use OOF CMO as the headline and show raw top-ticket overperformance alongside
  it.
- State the 2014 and cross-cycle limitations prominently.

### 3. The white-rural bundle

Use Morrow, Means, McLaughlin, Lindsey, Letson, Harper, Ledbetter, and White to distinguish issue
dimensions:

- cultural conservatism on guns and abortion;
- economic populism and public-school commitments;
- local accessibility and long constituent relationships;
- differentiation from national Democratic branding.

Means supplies the strongest causal-warning vignette: the same ideological
figure moved from weak 2010 CMO during an indictment to strong 2014 CMO after
acquittal and a local mayoral comeback.

Use Ledbetter's 2010 Democratic candidacy, party switch, and 2014 Republican
victory as the clearest realignment sequence. Pair it with David Beddingfield's
positive 2014 CMO and resource disadvantage in the same district, while being
explicit that Beddingfield's issue platform remains missing.

### 4. Realignment created overperformance without changing the candidate

Use Letson and other repeat candidates. Their legislative margins sometimes
changed much less than the statewide Democratic baseline. As the top of the
ticket collapsed, a surviving local vote became a much larger overperformance.

The key idea: **some enormous CMO values represent a personal vote holding still
while the party moved underneath it.**

### 5. The Black-majority counterexample

Use Anthony Daniels, Barbara Boyd, and Vivian Davis Figures. Boyd and Figures
were on the more progressive side of the Alabama Democratic caucus and
nevertheless generated large positive CMO
and raw-overperformance values. Explain why Black turnout, community networks,
incumbency, and weak Republican recruitment are distinct mechanisms from white
crossover. Add David Burkette as the organizational-network case: a public
educator with AEA and Alabama Democratic Conference support, deep local elected
experience, and documented campaign-management problems. His result helps
distinguish community familiarity from either ideological conservatism or
polished campaign execution.

Figures supplies the strongest campaign-time counterexample: she sponsored a
firearm safe-storage bill in 2010 and still produced positive CMO. Pair that
ideological evidence with her long incumbency and the Figures family network.

### 6. The suburban counterexample

Use Alli Summerford and Felicia Stewart. Their platforms emphasized education,
health care, ethics, environmental protection, or criticism of culture-war
priorities. They show that a contextual model can identify candidate strength
without the candidate moving right, though both ran behind the unusually strong
2018 Democratic statewide context on the raw comparison.

### 7. Put the simple theory on trial

Present the systematic checks:

- near-zero overall rank relationship between Shor--McCarty ideology and OOF
  CMO;
- opposite 2010 and 2014 cycle relationships;
- a 2014-excluded association that is negative, not positive, across total,
  district-grouped, and resource-adjusted CMO specifications;
- five scored matched pairs: higher-CMO candidate more conservative once, more
  progressive twice, and ideologically similar twice;
- Lindsey versus Robinson and Lindsey versus Curtis as cases where large CMO
  differences exist without broad ideological differences;
- simple years of tenure also have near-zero association.
- explain the temporal audit: later governing positions are corroboration, not
  evidence available to voters in the earlier election.

### 8. The synthesis

Replace “moderation wins” with **district-congruent differentiation**:

> Candidates overperformed when they possessed a credible identity that fit the
> district and was stronger than the national party cue. What constituted fit
> differed across rural white, Black-majority, and suburban districts.

### 9. End with 2026 relevance

Do not turn the historical analysis into a forecast coefficient. Instead ask
what candidate recruitment should look for:

- real local networks rather than generic ideological labels;
- issue bundles fitted to the district;
- credible public-school, health-care, and economic messages;
- opponents and campaign resources as part of the race, not noise around the
  candidate.

## Planned visuals

1. **CMO versus Shor--McCarty score:** candidate-cycle scatter, colored by cycle
   and faceted by district coalition; label the strongest cases.
   Add full-model geography-sensitivity intervals, especially for Daniels,
   Drummond, Ford, Summerford, and Stewart, rather than plotting production CMO
   as exact.
2. **The party moved underneath them:** repeat-candidate slopegraph showing
   legislative margin, statewide baseline, raw overperformance, and CMO by
   cycle.
3. **Issue-bundle heatmap:** guns, abortion, economic, labor, party independence,
   and localism codes for researched candidates.
4. **Matched-pair dumbbells:** CMO gap beside ideology-score gap for the five
   pairs with scores on both sides.
5. **Three coalition case cards:** Morrow/Means, Boyd/Daniels, and
   Summerford/Stewart.

## Claims that still require stronger evidence

- Contemporaneous abortion and gun positions for Lindsey, Letson, White, Irons,
  and McLaughlin beyond broad descriptions or later scores.
- Pre-election opponent-quality profiles for each primary matched pair.
- A source-grounded definition of constituency service/local networks rather
  than tenure alone.
- Additional contemporaneous cultural-issue positions for Andy Betterton; his
  2014 economic platform is now verified through the site's publication metadata.
- Sensitivity of Black-majority raw overperformance to alternative statewide
  baselines and turnout composition.
- Better post-2014 ideology coverage: only three clean Shor--McCarty matches
  are available in 2018, and the source ends before the 2022 legislature.
