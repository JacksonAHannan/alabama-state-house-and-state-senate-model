# Linda Meigs

## Why she matters

Linda Meigs recorded about `+15.1` out-of-fold CMO in House District 20 in 2018. Her resource-adjusted score is lower, about `+9.7`, so campaign resources may explain part of the result. She is still useful as a suburban, non-incumbent comparison to the older rural personal-vote cases.

## Campaign-time ideological evidence

The Huntsville Times's October 2018 voter guide lists Medicaid expansion and an education lottery among Meigs's principal priorities. She connected Medicaid expansion to hospital closures, infant mortality, mental-health and addiction services, and diabetes treatment. BallotReady's preserved links to her campaign platform add support for early-literacy and pre-K funding, dual enrollment and skilled-trades training, green-energy jobs, replacing the regressive grocery sales tax, and a statewide living wage. This is a coherent public-investment platform, although it still does not establish her positions on guns or most cultural issues.

## Competing explanation

Meigs was a retired Grissom High School teacher and had participated in local Democratic organizations, giving her a recognizable education and community profile. But she was a first-time candidate. The surviving evidence supports some occupational credibility, not the kind of decades-long personal electoral organization visible in the strongest incumbent cases.

Her approximately 5.5-point decline after resource adjustment also cautions against attributing all of her CMO to ideology or biography.

## Interpretation

Meigs is another case that does not fit a culturally conservative crossover story. Her recovered platform emphasized public investment and health-care access. Her result is more compatible with suburban coalition change, education credibility, and campaign resources, although the evidence is too sparse to rank those mechanisms confidently.
