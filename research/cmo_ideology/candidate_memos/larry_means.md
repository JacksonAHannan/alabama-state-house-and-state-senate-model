# Larry Means: culturally conservative, economically populist, locally resilient

## Electoral pattern

Larry Means is a particularly valuable within-candidate case because his broad
ideology appears stable while his electoral circumstances changed sharply.

| Cycle | Status | Result | OOF CMO | District-grouped CMO | Raw overperformance |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2010 | Democratic incumbent | lost | -5.7 | -4.0 | +14.0 |
| 2014 | former senator/rematch challenger | lost narrowly | +29.2 | +31.8 | +28.2 |

The 2014 whole-cycle holdout score is much larger still, but cross-cycle model
validation is poor and that estimate should not be treated as precise.

## Ideological profile

Means fits the classic heterogeneous Alabama Democratic bundle better than a
one-dimensional moderate label:

- **Guns:** strongly conservative. Vote Smart records a 100 percent NRA-PVF
  Alabama rating, and Means sponsored the NRA-supported legislation that became
  Alabama's 2006 stand-your-ground law.
- **Economics and labor:** populist/developmentalist rather than conventionally
  conservative. His campaign emphasized jobs and public education. A
  contemporary working-families column credited him with helping create an
  industrial authority that preserved 1,400 Goodyear jobs and added 190.
- **Localism:** exceptionally strong. Before the Senate he had served in Attalla
  government for years. After losing the Senate seat and being acquitted in the
  bingo-corruption trials, he returned to win a four-way Attalla mayoral race
  with 69 percent.
- **Broad roll-call position:** his Shor--McCarty score of `+0.143` places him at
  the conservative edge of the Alabama Democratic caucus in 2014.

## The crucial competing explanation

Means was indicted roughly one month before the 2010 general election in the
federal bingo-corruption case. He was later acquitted in two trials. By 2012 he
had restored his standing sufficiently to win the Attalla mayoralty decisively,
and in 2014 he again ran competitively against Republican Phil Williams.

That chronology makes the within-candidate swing more naturally interpretable
as **scandal shock followed by local reputational recovery** than as ideological
movement. His gun position and general roll-call ideology did not need to change
to produce very different CMO results.

## Article use

Means should be a central article vignette because he supports two propositions
at once:

1. culturally conservative issue positioning was genuinely part of the
   overperforming rural Democratic coalition; and
2. ideology alone cannot explain when that coalition materialized.

The case is strongest when framed as a bundle of gun conservatism, economic
localism, long public service, and reputation--not as evidence that simply
moving right generated 29 CMO points.
