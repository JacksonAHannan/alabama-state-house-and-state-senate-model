# Nathaniel Ledbetter

## Why he matters

Nathaniel Ledbetter posted about `+22.1` OOF CMO as the Democratic candidate for House District 24 in 2010. After changing parties, he won the same seat as a Republican in 2014 and eventually became Speaker of the Alabama House.

## Ideological evidence

A memorandum describing a 2013 party-switch meeting reports that Ledbetter called himself conservative and identified opposition to abortion and gay rights as the basis for that label. Because the discussion occurred after the scored election and came through an interested participant, it is retrospective corroboration rather than evidence of what 2010 voters encountered.

The same memorandum says Ledbetter believed most of his former Democratic voters would remain with him after the switch. That is unusually direct evidence for a personal vote crossing party labels, even though it records his expectation rather than observed voter motives.

## Competing explanation

Ledbetter had served on the Rainsville City Council and as mayor well before the legislative campaign. Deep municipal roots are inseparable from his ideological and partisan evolution.

## Interpretation

Ledbetter is one of the strongest qualitative examples of a culturally conservative Democrat whose support was candidate-centered. He still cannot establish that conservatism alone caused his 2010 overperformance: local officeholding and recognition provide an equally plausible mechanism.
