# Billy Beasley

## Why he matters

Billy Beasley recorded about `+17.2` OOF CMO when he moved from the Alabama House to an open Senate District 28 seat in 2010.

## Ideological evidence

Beasley's Shor–McCarty score from his pre-election House service was `-0.217`, around the 64th conservative percentile of the contemporary Democratic Senate reference distribution. That makes him moderately conservative within the caucus, but not an ideological-edge case.

The research pipeline previously labeled this observation “post-election or other chamber only,” conflating two different situations. It now explicitly recognizes pre-election service in the other legislative chamber as temporally valid broad ideology evidence.

## Competing explanation

Beasley was already a multi-term state representative and defeated former Tuskegee mayor Johnny Ford in a racially charged Democratic runoff before facing Republican Kim West. Prior officeholding and the primary coalition are plausible sources of recognition independent of ideological valence.

## Interpretation

Beasley is consistent with modest conservative congruence but offers little support for a strong moderation effect. His established legislative identity and the unusual runoff context should remain foregrounded.
