# John Robinson

## Why he matters

John Robinson is the lower-CMO member of the strongest same-era ideology match.
He had represented House District 23 since 1994 but recorded about `+4.5` Total
OOF CMO in 2014 and lost to Republican Tommy Hanes. Richard Lindsey recorded
about `+40.8` CMO despite occupying a similar ideological band.

## Ideological evidence

Robinson's pre-election Shor–McCarty score places him around the 87th conservative
percentile of Alabama Democratic House members, close to Lindsey's 92nd percentile.
AEA records document substantial campaign support, while the Alabama Retail
Association also endorsed him. This combination suggests conservative relative-
caucus voting alongside cross-institutional business and education support.

No specific time-valid gun or abortion position has been recovered. Republican
campaign material calling him liberal or an “AEA crony” is retained only as an
opponent perspective, not as an ideology code.

## Opponent and resources

Hanes was a retired firefighter and commercial fisherman with deep family roots
in the area. His campaign emphasized pro-life, pro-family, education, and Second
Amendment themes. FollowTheMoney reports approximately `$125,109` for Hanes and
`$108,569` for Robinson—a competitive financial contest, unlike Lindsey's race
against a severely underfunded challenger.

## Interpretation

The Lindsey–Robinson comparison is evidence against a simple ideology gradient.
Both Democrats were conservative relative to their caucus and education-aligned;
their CMO scores nevertheless differed by about 36 points. Opponent quality,
resources, and the strength of each incumbent's local network are better candidates
for explaining the gap.
