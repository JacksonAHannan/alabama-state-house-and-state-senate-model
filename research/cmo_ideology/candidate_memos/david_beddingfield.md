# David Beddingfield

## Why he matters

David Beddingfield recorded about `+23.2` OOF CMO in House District 24 in 2014 against Nathaniel Ledbetter. This creates a potentially valuable same-district sequence: Ledbetter overperformed as a Democrat in 2010, switched parties, and then faced another Democratic candidate four years later.

## Evidence status

No detailed issue platform has yet been recovered for Beddingfield. The renewed
archive search is logged in `source_provenance_audit.csv`. AEA records
show education-union support during the general-election campaign, supplying a
modest labor/public-education alignment signal. Contemporary local reporting
identifies him as the Democratic nominee and reports about $60,000 in
contributions, compared with roughly $145,000 for Ledbetter. His positive CMO
therefore occurred despite a substantial resource disadvantage.

An official 2008 Alabama school directory identifies Beddingfield as president
of the Fort Payne City Board of Education, and an April 2014 civil-rights letter
still copies him in that role. His long school-governance tenure supplies a
powerful local-recognition and education-network mechanism that predates the
campaign.

## Interpretation

The 2010–2014 HD-24 sequence helps distinguish candidate loyalty, party switching,
and district realignment, but it still cannot test cultural or economic ideology
because Beddingfield's full platform is missing. The evidence now supports local
education credibility, rather than ideological moderation, as the leading
documented mechanism.
