# Jerry Fielding

## Why he matters

Jerry Fielding recorded about `+18.2` out-of-fold CMO while winning Senate District 11 as a Democrat in 2010. He switched to the Republican Party in 2012 and later presented himself as culturally conservative, making him tempting—but potentially misleading—evidence for ideological moderation.

## Ideological evidence

No reliable campaign-time 2010 issue platform or pre-election legislative record has been recovered. Fielding did not enter the Senate until the election being explained, so his Shor–McCarty score is post-election evidence. His 2012 party-switch statement opposed same-sex marriage, abortion, and gun control, but it came almost two years after the race and amid redistricting that made his seat more Republican.

Those later positions are biographically relevant but excluded from the 2010 ideology codes. His 2010 ideological valence remains unknown.

## Competing explanations

Fielding had served for 26 years as a Talladega County district and circuit judge. Contemporary September 2010 reporting identified him as a retired circuit judge, giving him unusually strong district-wide recognition and a public-service identity independent of party.

The Republican campaign was severely disrupted. Longtime senator Jim Preuitt, who had recently switched from Democrat to Republican, abandoned his reelection bid on September 1. The Republican Party selected Talladega businessman Ray Robbins as the replacement nominee on September 7, leaving roughly eight weeks before Election Day. Fielding therefore faced neither the incumbent nor a candidate emerging through a normal primary campaign.

The Alabama Police Benevolent Association also endorsed Fielding. Its published winners list appeared after Election Day, so it confirms organizational support but is not treated as a time-eligible issue statement.

## Interpretation

Fielding is better evidence for local public-service valence and opponent disruption than for ideological conservatism. His later cultural positions may have been longstanding, but the surviving record cannot prove that temporal link. Any article treatment should lead with his judicial career and the Republican substitution, then describe the ideology question as unresolved.
