# Dennis W. Stephens

## Why he matters

Dennis W. Stephens is the lower-CMO comparison for two large 2014 overperformers,
Jody Letson and Andy Betterton. Stephens recorded about `-5.4` Total OOF CMO in
House District 22, compared with approximately `+37.2` for Letson and `+27.5` for
Betterton. Understanding the comparison prevents the article from studying only
successful-looking cases.

## Time-valid evidence

Stephens signed the Alabama Anti-Corruption Pledge in May 2014. A contemporary
Jackson County election index also links to a Clarion video interview with him,
but the original page now returns 404 and its contents have not been recovered.
Neither item supports a code on economics, guns, abortion, labor, or social
ideology. His issue profile therefore remains unknown rather than centrist.

## Opponent and resources

Stephens faced Ritchie Whorton, who had just defeated incumbent Republican Wayne
Johnson by 58 to 42 percent in the primary. Whorton publicly attributed that win
to grassroots campaigning and a message opposing Montgomery political bosses,
and advocated smaller and more transparent government. Stephens also had a large
modeled spending disadvantage. The comparison is therefore confounded by a
well-organized, high-momentum Republican opponent and unequal resources.

## Interpretation

Stephens is not evidence that an insufficiently conservative Democrat failed.
His ideology is still unobserved. What the record does establish is that generic
anti-corruption positioning did not overcome a strong Republican campaign in a
deeply Republican district. That makes him a useful negative case for the broader
claim that local credibility must be relational and opponent-dependent.
