# Barbara A. Drummond

## Why she matters

Barbara Drummond recorded about `+29.9` OOF CMO in the 2014 House District 103
race. Her district-grouped estimate remains strongly positive at about `+27.5`.
She is both a high performer and the comparison candidate for Anthony Daniels,
whose still larger CMO cannot be explained by a more progressive profile.

Her exact magnitude is highly geography-sensitive. Refitting the full OOF model
under every audited precinct-allocation scenario produces a CMO range of about
`+12.9` to `+29.9`. She remains an overperformer in every scenario, but is not a
stable top-five case.

## Ideological valence

Drummond's Shor--McCarty score (`-0.610`) places her on the more progressive
side of Alabama's Democratic House caucus. Because she first entered the
legislature in the election being studied, that score is post-election evidence.
Her later record corroborates it: support for Medicaid expansion, opposition to
permitless carry, gun-storage legislation, public education, and equity-focused
neighborhood investment.

One campaign-visible signal has now been recovered: Alabama Education
Association records show support shortly before the 2014 general election.
That supplies a modest labor/public-education alignment code. The evidence does
not establish that her later positions were salient in 2014. They remain useful
for classifying her subsequent governing profile, not for claiming a
prospective ideological treatment.

## Competing explanations

Drummond entered politics with unusually deep Mobile networks: roughly two
decades as a local journalist followed by county and city administrative work.
Her career included neighborhood services, public works, constituent-response
systems, and participation in major industrial recruitment. HD-103 was also a
majority-Black district. Community familiarity and coalition mobilization are
therefore more plausible mechanisms than conservative crossover.

## Interpretation

Drummond is a clean counterexample to the universal move-right story. A
progressive governing profile and strong local administrative identity coexisted
with large CMO in a majority-Black district.
