# Barbara Bigsby Boyd: a progressive-caucus counterexample

## Electoral pattern

Barbara Bigsby Boyd is the strongest repeated counterexample to the proposition
that Alabama Democratic overperformance required ideological conservatism.

| Cycle | OOF CMO | District-grouped CMO | Raw overperformance | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2010 | +40.2 | +40.1 | +58.6 | incumbent; defeated Republican Ron Struzik |
| 2018 | +18.5 | +18.2 | +53.3 | incumbent; district about 60% nonwhite |
| 2022 | +14.8 | +15.1 | +56.3 | incumbent; district about 62% nonwhite |

Her 2014 election is excluded from CMO because no Republican appeared; she faced
only an independent candidate.

## Ideological and biographical profile

Boyd's Shor--McCarty score is `-0.504`, approximately the 32nd conservative
percentile of Alabama Democratic House members serving in 2010. In other words,
she was more progressive than roughly two-thirds of that caucus on the broad
roll-call dimension.

That placement fits her biography better than a conservative-Democrat story.
Boyd worked in education for more than 45 years and represented HD-32 from 1994
onward. An official legislative commendation describes her as a consistent
advocate for education, children, seniors, and local communities.

## Mechanism

Boyd's overperformance is unlikely to represent white conservative crossover.
Later-cycle HD-32 estimates are approximately 60--62 percent nonwhite. The more
plausible mechanisms are:

- a very long incumbent personal vote;
- educator and community networks;
- stronger down-ballot Black support or turnout than the same-cycle statewide
  Democratic ticket received in the district;
- weak Republican recruitment in a district where the legislative office had
  been locally anchored to Boyd for decades.

The raw-versus-baseline pattern is particularly striking. In 2010 the modeled
top-of-ticket baseline was about Republican +15.9, yet Boyd won the legislative
vote by Democratic +42.7. Similar raw gaps recur in 2018 and 2022. That is a
coalition and officeholding story, not evidence of conservative ideological
valence.

## Article use

Boyd should appear immediately after the white-rural examples. Her case prevents
the article from turning a mechanism that may apply to Morrow, Means, or
McLaughlin into a statewide law. A defensible formulation is:

> Cultural conservatism helped some white-rural Democrats maintain crossover
> support, while long-serving Black Democrats generated a different kind of
> overperformance through community and down-ballot coalition strength.

The remaining question is how much of the enormous raw gap reflects Boyd's
personal vote versus systematic weakness in using statewide executive returns
as the baseline for Black-majority legislative districts.
