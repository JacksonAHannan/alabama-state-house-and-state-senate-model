# Napoleon Bracy Jr.

## Why he matters

Napoleon Bracy recorded about `+20.7` out-of-fold CMO while winning House District 98 in 2010. His fundraising-adjusted score is lower, approximately `+12.9`, but remains positive.

## Ideological evidence

No campaign-time issue platform or pre-election roll-call record has been recovered. Bracy's Shor–McCarty score is on the progressive side of the later Alabama Democratic caucus, but his observed legislative service begins after the election being explained. It is therefore excluded from the 2010 ideology analysis.

This is an important substantive missing value. Bracy should not be classified as progressive, moderate, or conservative in 2010 based on later votes.

## Competing explanations

Bracy entered the legislative race with six years of Prichard City Council service and had been selected council president. That is a substantial local-office foundation. The best contemporary institutional notice was published in December 2010, however, so it documents a preexisting fact after the election rather than providing time-eligible campaign evidence.

Resources also mattered. FollowTheMoney reports roughly `$100,000` for Bracy and `$12,000` for the Republican. Accounting for that advantage reduces CMO by nearly eight points. His majority-Black district and municipal base remain plausible additional mechanisms.

## Interpretation

Bracy is evidence against filling archival silence with ideology. His result is consistent with local-office experience, fundraising, and coalition-specific down-ballot voting. Until a 2010 platform, questionnaire, endorsement record, or contemporaneous interview is recovered, the ideological mechanism remains unknown.
