# Alan Harper

## Why he matters

Alan Harper recorded about `+24.1` OOF CMO as an incumbent Democrat in House District 61 in 2010 and switched to the Republican Party in 2012. He is the cohort's clearest party-switching test case.

## Ideological evidence

Harper's pre-election Shor–McCarty score was `-0.105`, around the 71st conservative percentile of the active Alabama Democratic House caucus. This places him on the caucus's conservative side, though not at an extreme.

His later switch is consistent with conservative or Republican constituency fit, but it is not itself pre-election evidence. Harper said his priorities—business, education, jobs, and district welfare—had not changed. Democratic leader Craig Ford countered that joining the Republican supermajority would require Harper to change substantive votes. The episode should therefore be treated as corroboration with ambiguity, not proof of why voters supported him in 2010.

## Competing explanation

The switch announcement also describes a long local-government, education, banking, and economic-development career and substantial job-recruitment claims. Local service and incumbency are plausible independent sources of overperformance.

## Interpretation

Harper is compatible with the conservative-Democrat hypothesis, but he does not isolate it. His case is best presented beside more progressive local overperformers and with explicit controls for incumbency and local tenure.
