# David “Coach” Burkette

## Why he matters

David Burkette posted about `+23.5` OOF CMO in Senate District 26 in 2018. His case offers unusually clear evidence that community networks and down-ballot loyalty can coexist with weak conventional campaign operations.

## Ideological evidence

Before the 2018 general election, Burkette called repairing the public education system a top priority. The Alabama Education Association also supported his campaign. Together these justify modest economically progressive and labor-aligned codes, but the evidence is too narrow to characterize his social ideology.

## Competing explanations

Burkette grew up in the district, worked in its public schools as a coach and administrator, and served three terms on the Montgomery City Council. Contemporary coverage attributed his special-primary upset partly to this history. The Alabama Democratic Conference endorsement supplied another organizational route to voters.

At the same time, detailed contemporary reporting described campaign debt, unpaid workers, very low cash on hand, and management conflict. That cuts against explaining the result as superior campaign execution. The stronger interpretation is personal and organizational familiarity in a heavily Democratic Montgomery district.

## Interpretation

Burkette is not clean evidence that ideological moderation produced overperformance. His documented economic positioning leaned toward public education and organized labor, while local identity and Democratic organizational networks provide stronger mechanisms.
