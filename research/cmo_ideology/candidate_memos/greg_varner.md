# Greg Varner

## Why he matters

Greg Varner recorded about `+15.5` out-of-fold CMO while losing Senate District 13 to Gerald Dial by roughly one point in 2010. The race is an unusually useful warning about resources and party switching.

## Ideological evidence

One direct 2010 economic position has now been recovered. In a June 1 Anniston Star report, Varner said his main goal was to strengthen small businesses by giving them the same kinds of tax breaks and incentives offered to larger industries. This is strong contemporaneous evidence of a small-business and tax-incentive priority, though it does not by itself locate him cleanly on a left-right scale. Varner was an Ashland attorney who had represented individuals, small businesses, family farms, and local governments, but a current professional biography cannot establish his broader 2010 ideological valence.

His cultural and social-issue positions remain unknown.

## Competing explanations

Varner had an enormous fundraising advantage. FollowTheMoney reports approximately `$1.14 million` for Varner and `$404,000` for Dial. Total OOF CMO is about `+15.5`; fundraising-adjusted CMO falls to about `+8.4`. The financial advantage plausibly explains nearly half of the headline residual.

Dial was not a generic Republican. Contemporary local reporting noted that he had held the seat for years as a Democrat before switching parties. Varner was therefore challenging a long-tenured opponent with an inherited Democratic personal vote during the 2010 realignment.

## Interpretation

Varner cannot presently support a broad ideological claim. His case belongs in the competing-explanations section: substantial money helped a Democrat remain competitive, while the Republican opponent carried unusual tenure and cross-party recognition. The recovered small-business parity message supplies a plausible locally resonant economic theme, but the residual left after fundraising adjustment may also reflect Varner's local legal practice or other campaign attributes.
