# Tammy Irons

## Why she matters

Tammy Irons recorded about `+28.0` OOF CMO in the 2010 open-seat Senate District
1 contest. The district-grouped estimate is about `+29.7`, making the result
robust to the principal historical scoring alternatives.

## Ideological valence

Irons is not a strong conservative-Democrat case. Her pre-election
Shor--McCarty score (`-0.437`) places her around the middle of Alabama Democrats
serving in that era. The issue record recovered so far tilts toward public
investment and the education establishment: university grants during 2010, a
bill supported by the Alabama Education Association in 2008, and a later effort
to increase teachers' classroom-supply stipends.

No sufficiently strong contemporaneous evidence has yet been found on abortion
or guns. Those dimensions remain `NA`; silence is not coded as moderation.

## Competing explanations

Irons was already an elected House member and Florence attorney with local
civic ties. She had won the district in a 2006 special election and again that
November, giving her an existing political network before she sought the open
Senate seat. Her performance could therefore reflect transfer of a House-level
personal vote, not an ideological effect.

## Interpretation

Irons weakens a simple cultural-conservatism thesis. The available evidence
shows a middle-of-caucus legislator with a strong education orientation and an
established local political base. Further archival work is needed before making
any claim about her social ideology.
