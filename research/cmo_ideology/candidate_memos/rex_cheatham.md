# Rex Cheatham

## Why he matters

Rex Cheatham recorded about `+16.7` out-of-fold CMO against Republican incumbent Terri Collins in House District 8 in 2014. His score is stable across the resource specifications: approximately `+16.0` after adjusting for state-file spending and `+17.7` after adjusting for FollowTheMoney fundraising.

## Campaign-time ideological evidence

The strongest recovered evidence concerns organized education rather than cultural issues. A 2012 University of North Alabama alumni profile documents that Cheatham had taught in Morgan County schools for ten years, worked for the Alabama Education Association, served as education liaison to Governor George Wallace, and led a gubernatorial education-reform committee. Contemporary 2014 reporting also identified him as a former AEA employee.

This supports a strong education-labor alignment code. It does not establish Cheatham's positions on abortion, guns, taxation, health care, or other social issues. Those dimensions remain unknown.

## Competing explanations

Cheatham had deep local and party networks. The same profile reports local and statewide civic awards, leadership of the Morgan County Democratic Executive Committee, and repeated service as a Democratic national and state delegate. His education career supplied both occupational credibility and an organizational base.

Money does not explain the residual. FollowTheMoney reports roughly `$87,000` for Cheatham versus `$273,000` for Collins, while the state-file spending feature also favors Collins. Both adjusted CMO measures remain strongly positive.

## Interpretation

Cheatham is evidence for an education-labor version of district congruence, not for culturally conservative moderation. His recovered public identity centered on schools, AEA experience, and local Democratic organization. Because no cultural-issue platform has been recovered, he should not be used to claim that progressive or conservative social positioning caused the result.
