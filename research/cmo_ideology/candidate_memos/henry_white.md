# Henry A. White

## Why he matters

Henry White is a useful repeat-candidate test. In House District 5, his OOF CMO
moved from about `-4.4` in 2010 to `+29.7` in 2014. The district-grouped and
resource-adjusted estimates are nearly identical. Both races were against Dan
Williams, which removes one major source of case-to-case variation.

## Ideological valence

White's pre-election Shor--McCarty score was `-0.230`, near the 79th
conservative percentile of the Alabama Democratic House caucus. That supports
a relatively conservative *overall* roll-call profile, but the clearest
contemporary campaign material is economically populist rather than fiscally
conservative. His 2014 message emphasized public schools, jobs, locally owned
businesses, industrial recruitment, and help for elderly and disabled people.

## Personal-vote evidence

The same campaign profile describes a lifelong Limestone County resident with
36 years in the county schools, including four as superintendent; six years on
the Athens City Council; prior legislative service; and local civic work. This
is unusually strong evidence for a locally rooted professional network.

## Interpretation

White fits the broader white-rural bundle: a relatively conservative caucus
record combined with public-school advocacy and deep local roots. But his
within-person CMO shift cannot be attributed to ideology alone. The candidate
and opponent were the same in 2010 and 2014, while White's CMO moved by roughly
34 points. His documented political identity is much more plausibly stable than
that change. The electoral environment, district baseline, campaign execution,
and the meaning of the Democratic label changed around him. This is unusually
strong within-person evidence against treating CMO as a direct ideology score.
