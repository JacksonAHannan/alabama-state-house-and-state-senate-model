# Richard Lindsey

## Why he matters

Richard Lindsey recorded about `+40.8` Total OOF CMO while winning House District
39 in 2014. His matched comparison with John Robinson is especially valuable:
both were long-serving white rural Democratic incumbents with similar pre-election
roll-call ideology, yet Robinson recorded only about `+4.5` CMO and lost.

## Ideological evidence

Lindsey's Shor–McCarty score places him around the 92nd conservative percentile
of the Alabama Democratic House caucus. AEA campaign-finance records also show
substantial support during the general-election period. His observable bundle is
therefore conservative relative to fellow Democrats while aligned with the
education establishment—not a uniformly small-government profile.

No time-valid evidence has yet been recovered for Lindsey's abortion or gun
positions. Those dimensions remain unknown.

## Competing explanations

Lindsey had represented the district since 1983 and previously chaired the
Education Finance and Appropriations Committee. His Republican opponent Heath
Jones raised only about `$5,891` in FollowTheMoney data and campaigned against
career politicians, Medicaid expansion, taxation, and special interests. The
state-file spending feature shows an overwhelming Lindsey advantage.

Resource adjustment reduces the Lindsey–Robinson CMO gap from about 36.3 to
31.4 points, so money explains part but not most of the difference. Lindsey's
31-year constituent network and his opponent's limited resources remain the
strongest documented mechanisms.

## Interpretation

Lindsey is compatible with ideological congruence, but the paired evidence shows
why it cannot be the sole explanation. John Robinson occupied nearly the same
broad ideological band and had comparable education-union alignment. Lindsey's
larger personal vote is better distinguished by tenure, institutional power,
resources, and opponent strength.
