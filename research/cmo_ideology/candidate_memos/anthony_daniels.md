# Anthony Daniels

## Why he matters

Anthony Daniels has the largest production OOF CMO in the cohort, about `+55.7`
in the 2014 House District 53 race. The district-grouped and resource-adjusted
scores remain above `+53`, so neither ordinary district leakage nor the modeled
spending advantage explains the result.

## Ideological evidence

No issue evidence available by the November 2014 election has yet been
recovered. A renewed 2026 archive search found the certified result, qualification
coverage, the RetailPAC endorsement, and later profiles, but no contemporaneous
platform or issue interview; that negative result is recorded in
`source_provenance_audit.csv`. A later institutional biography documents extensive roles that
predated the election: chairing the NEA Student Program, chairing the Huntsville
Education Association legislative committee, and serving on the Alabama
Education Association state board. These establish an education-labor identity,
but the surviving source was published later and is excluded from the
pre-election index. Daniels's later record is broadly progressive on Medicaid expansion,
abortion, and concealed-carry permits, and his Shor--McCarty score is not near
the most progressive edge of his caucus. Those facts classify his subsequent
governing profile but cannot be used as pre-election issue exposure. His 2014
issue dimensions therefore remain `NA` in the quantitative hand-coded table.

## Coalition and baseline sensitivity

HD-53 was approximately 70 percent nonwhite. Daniels won the two-party vote by
55.6 points, while the production top-ticket baseline is `-13.65`, yielding raw
overperformance of 69.2 points. That baseline is geographically sensitive. The
production weights rely more heavily on conservative county allocation; richer
unambiguous precinct-link scenarios produce a `-4.38` baseline. Across all
tested regimes the range is 9.27 points, and raw overperformance at the scenario
mean is 61.8 rather than 69.2.

The result remains enormous under either baseline, but its exact magnitude is
not stable. It likely captures coalition turnout, stronger Democratic loyalty
down ballot, local candidate and opponent effects, and geography uncertainty in
addition to candidate appeal.

## Opponent and resources

Republican Christian Horn received 22.2 percent. Later biographies show that he
was a business owner and Tennessee Valley Republican Club chair, so he was not
obviously a fictitious opponent, but contemporaneous evidence about the 2014
campaign's intensity is still missing. Daniels had a large spending advantage;
the resource-adjusted model nevertheless leaves nearly all of the CMO intact.
The Alabama Retail Association PAC also endorsed Daniels in 2014. Paired with
his education-association history, this suggests unusually broad institutional
acceptance rather than a narrowly ideological campaign identity.

## Interpretation

Daniels is a counterexample to a simple conservative-crossover story and a
warning about interpreting extreme CMO literally. His case belongs in the
Black-majority coalition section, with a visible geography-sensitivity bar and
no claim that ideology caused the result.
